Digital Transformation for the Global South · Master's thesis

How can digital transformation be strategically implemented to leverage sustainable development? Comparing Lebanon, Mexico and Ivory Coast, we found that digital tools have spread faster than the development they were meant to bring. In all three countries the missing piece was the same: coordination between sectors. And the people left out of digital services are the same people left out of their benefits, so governance, skills and basic access have to come first.

LebanonRebuild institutional capacity
MexicoConnect capacity that already exists, but in isolation
Ivory CoastFollow through on the gaps its own strategy names

A country can adopt a lot of technology and still transform very little. What makes the difference is how well it is coordinated.

MegaMind · a sovereign AI system, built from scratch

v0.5.1 · in progress
View on GitHub

What started as a curious Python experiment has become a first reasoning core. The goal: own the intelligence stack instead of relying on external AI models or APIs. It is still primitive, but it is becoming a real learning system rather than a chatbot.

What it can do today
Store knowledge that persists between sessions
Learn from its own experiences
Discover simple mathematical relationships
Compare strategies and remember which ones work
Form basic concepts and reinforce them over time
Keep its intelligence core separate from the interface
The next challenge: generalisation

Same idea, written four ways. Does MegaMind see that they mean the same thing?

Today, MegaMind recognises multiplication when it sees the * sign, but not when the same idea is expressed differently. The next step is to move its first "neuron" from recognising surface patterns to extracting concepts it can reuse in new situations.

Roadmap
  1. Python experimentFirst prototype
  2. Reasoning corev0.5.1, now
  3. GeneralisationConcepts, not patterns
  4. A first complete neuronLearns, abstracts, reuses
The long-term vision

Where MegaMind is heading: a sovereign architecture in three phases. A seed of verifiable logic grows into industry brains, which feed one central brain through federated learning. Each organisation keeps its private data at home; only what is learned gets shared. One organisation's progress becomes everyone's.

Energy development profiles · k-means clustering & linear regression

Python · 164 countries · 2022 data
View on GitHub

Energy is one of the first conditions for development, and a question of sovereignty. Using public data on 164 countries, I asked two questions: which countries share the same energy profile, and how closely electricity follows wealth.

    What the data says
    4 profilesK-means finds four families of countries, from abundant and fossil-based to scarce and low-carbon.
    22 countrieshave clean electricity but very little of it. A green mix is not the same as access: their challenge is volume.
    R² 0.83Income explains 83% of the differences in electricity per person. 1% more GDP goes with about 1.25% more electricity.

    Method: electricity generated per person and low-carbon share (renewables + nuclear), standardised, then k-means for k = 2 to 7; k = 4 has the best silhouette score. Data: Our World in Data (Ember, Energy Institute, Maddison Project). Countries far above the line, like Paraguay or Laos, are electricity exporters.
