    k-means clustering & linear regression · Python · 164 countries · 2022

    Energy development profiles

    Energy is one of the first conditions for development, and a question of sovereignty. Which countries share the same energy profile, and how closely does electricity follow wealth?

    Read the analysis ↗
      4 profilesk-means finds four families of countries, from abundant and fossil-based to scarce and low-carbon.
      22 countrieshave clean electricity but very little of it. A green mix is not the same as access.
      R² 0.83Income explains 83% of the differences in electricity per person. 1% more GDP goes with about 1.25% more.

      In simple words: every dot is a country. I let the computer sort 164 countries into four families, by how much electricity each person gets and how clean it is, to see who is on the same path and whether richer countries really have more power. Find yours. Data: Our World in Data.

      What it shows
      • A clean mix is not access. 22 countries, like Kenya, Ethiopia and Nepal, run on mostly low-carbon power but give each person under 300 kWh a year. Their challenge is volume, not the transition.
      • Electricity follows wealth. 1% more GDP per person goes with about 1.25% more electricity, and income alone explains 83% of the gaps between countries.
      • Outliers tell trade stories. Hydropower exporters like Paraguay and Laos sit far above the line; importers like Luxembourg sit below it.
      Why it matters · what's next
      • For the regions I care about, the priority is more power per person, and owning how it is produced.
      • Next: add access rates, reliability and prices, use consumption instead of generation, and follow each country over time.
